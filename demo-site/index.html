Look inside a vision model, on your own pictures.

Drop in a photo or a video frame. See the features a real model picks out and where each one fires. Then switch one off and watch the model's view of the scene change. It runs in your browser, with nothing to install.

Open the playgroundRun it on your model

  1. Bring a picture

    Upload one, use your camera or start from a robot frame. Pictures never leave your device.

  2. See what it notices

    A dictionary splits the model's view into features, like the edge of a table or a white ceiling, and shows where each fires.

  3. Change its mind

    Switch a feature off or turn it up, and the rest of the model runs again on the edit.

Then point it at your model

The same loop runs on your laptop, on any Hugging Face vision model and your own clips. A feature gets a tick only when it passes checks against random look-alikes and an untrained copy of the model.

Run it on your model

uv run ii run hf \
  --model <your-model> \
  --clips <your-clips> --steer

From the blog

A feature tracked our robot's failures. The control said it wasn't the cause. Switching off feature 720 in NVIDIA's GR00T dropped success from 39/75 to 1/9. Random features did that too. How ii kept us from calling it a finding.

ii is free, local and open on GitHub.
