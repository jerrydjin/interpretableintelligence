Research

A robot world model ignores its own gripper. We looked inside to see why.

Meta's V-JEPA 2-AC plans robot arm moves by imagining the next frame and scoring how close it gets to a goal picture. We used ii to look inside its predictor and ask one narrow question: does it take the gripper into account?

The blind spot

We found 119 moments in 68 DROID episodes where the gripper opens or closes while the arm moves less than 3 cm. With the arm step fixed, the planning score preferred the right gripper command 54% of the time (95% interval 45-63%). That is a coin flip. An untrained predictor scores 39%, because it always prefers one direction.

Where the command goes

The predictor reads the command once. After block 1, copying the action token from the right-command run into the wrong one restores 0% of the difference, and copying the frame tokens restores 97%.

Then the effect fades. Against a 6 cm arm step, the gripper's effect grows to 1.50 times the arm's at block 9, then falls to 0.28 by the output. In an untrained predictor it stays between 1.7 and 2.0 the whole way, so the fade is learned.

Looking for what turns it down

  • A sparse dictionary at block 9 has a latent that moves with the command on 75% of held-out events, against 65% for the best of 5 untrained dictionaries. Setting it and the next two latents to their right-command values changes the final prediction by 0%.
  • Attention in blocks 10, 12 and 14 each removes 9-16% of the gripper difference it receives, while adding 6-10% to the arm's. Each half of the episodes picked them on its own.
  • Knocking the top parts out recovered none of what was lost (-7%), the same as random parts (-7%). With every attention output after block 9 knocked out, the difference stays up to 33% larger, then the last two blocks bring it back to where it was (-0.2% recovered).
  • Adding the block-9 effect back at the output raised the right-command preference to 59% (49-68%). That is above every random direction (45-54%), but short of the 60% bar we set before the run.

What we think is going on

A probe on the encoder reads gripper open or closed on new episodes at AUROC 0.53, about the same as raw pixels (0.52). The predictor is trained to match those latents, and they barely change when the gripper does. Our reading is that the model learned the gripper hardly matters to its target, and its last blocks enforce that however we edit the middle. If so, the fix is a view of the gripper the encoder can see, not an edit inside the predictor. We have not tested that.

None of this is a finding. The dictionary latent passed its own check by a small margin. The knockouts and the steering did not pass. It is all offline, on recorded video, one planning step ahead.
