Research

A feature tracked our robot's failures. The control said it wasn't the cause.

We pointed ii at GR00T N1.7, NVIDIA's robot policy, on LIBERO-plus task 1028, a kitchen task it completes about half the time. Does one learned feature inside it make it fail?

What we did

  1. Recorded 100 episodes (56 successes) and captured transformer block 5 of the action model on its final denoise step.
  2. Trained a sparse dictionary on it: 8,192 features, 32 active at a time, held-out explained variance 0.998.
  3. Picked the features that fire more in the moments before a failure. Two stood out: feature 720 and the pair 7081+5852.
  4. Ran the policy again with each candidate switched off, on shared seeds, with a stop rule that ends an arm as soon as the answer is clear.

Before any of that, a sanity check: putting the dictionary's reconstruction in place of the real activations, with nothing switched off, left the actions identical to the baseline in 8 of 8 episodes. So the hook itself does not move the policy.

It looked like the answer

Switching feature 720 off dropped success from 39/75 to 1/9. The stop rule called it a fall after 9 episodes.

Then the control

Next to every candidate we switched off random features matched to it. For feature 720, 3 of 10 matched random features also made the policy fail. Across both candidates, 5 of 20 random controls fell.

Breaking a policy is easy. Many edits make it worse, so a drop when you remove a feature only says the policy uses that part of its activations. If feature 720 caused failures, switching it off should have raised success. That was the test, and neither candidate passed it. Both stay real=false.

What we changed in ii

The result shaped the tool. A feature now counts as a finding on a robot policy only when all of these hold on shared seeds:

  • The stop rule calls a rise in success, not a fall.
  • The rise over baseline is significant (McNemar p < 0.05).
  • It beats every matched random-feature control.
  • It beats an untrained dictionary run through the same picker, so the feature has to do better than what random weights would produce.

ii rollouts with --pick runs all four. The ablation in this post ran on the repo's earlier runner (ablate_v9.py), before the untrained-dictionary control shipped.

Every number behind this post

641 rollouts in total. Baseline: 39/75 (52%), Wilson 95% interval [0.4087, 0.6293]. Feature 720: 1/9, verdict fall. The 7081+5852 pair: 23/63 (37%), verdict fall.

Stop rule: Sobel–Wald. Stop when the rise test or the fall test accepts H1, or both accept H0, or at 75 episodes. Rise compares 0.56 with 0.86. Fall compares 0.56 with 0.26. Alpha is 0.05. Beta is 0.05. 0.56 is the capture success rate, 56/100. The verdict is rise when the rise test accepts H1, fall when the fall test accepts H1, and no_effect when both accept H0. At the cap with no decision, the verdict is cap_undecided.

Every condition

Conditions share seeds. Paired change is against baseline on the shared seeds. From demo-data/groot/groot-1028-ablate-summary.json.

The Wilson interval assumes the number of episodes was fixed in advance. Each SPRT condition stopped as soon as its boundary was crossed, so the sequential column is the exact 95% interval under that stopping rule, valid however early the run stopped. It is usually wider, and it is the one to read for the ablated conditions.

Feature 720: 3 of 10 matched random controls also fell, and 1 of 10 did at least as badly (permutation p = 0.182, Holm 0.364). Against p0 = 0.56 the sequential fall p is 0.012 (Holm 0.024) and the rise p is 0.993 (Holm 1.000). A drop below p0 that random features also produce is not specific to this feature.

The 7081+5852 pair: 2 of 10 matched random controls also fell, and 2 of 10 did at least as badly (permutation p = 0.273, Holm 0.364). Against p0 = 0.56 the sequential fall p is 0.039 (Holm 0.039) and the rise p is 0.961 (Holm 1.000). A drop below p0 that random features also produce is not specific to this feature.

With 10 controls per arm the permutation p-value cannot go below 0.091. Holm is across the 2 candidates. None of these numbers sets a verdict.
