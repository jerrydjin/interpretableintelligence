Look inside a vision model. Then change its mind.

This is DINOv2-small, the kind of vision model robots use to see. It runs right here in your browser. Give it any picture and see the things it has learnt to notice, then switch one off and watch how its view of the scene changes.

How this works

  1. The model looks

    It cuts the picture into a 16 by 16 grid of patches and describes every patch with a list of 384 numbers. We read those numbers halfway through the model.

  2. A dictionary splits them into features

    A dictionary trained on 80 robot videos breaks each description into a few features out of 3,072, each one a thing the model notices, such as a white ceiling or the edge of the frame. An AI named some of them from where they fire.

  3. Tests decide the tick

    A name is a guess until it passes four checks. Only features that pass every one get a green tick: 5 of 3,072 so far.

    What are the checks?
    • It fires across many different videos, not just one.
    • Its name still fits videos that were held back while it was found.
    • Switching it off changes the model more than at least 95 of 100 random look-alike edits do.
    • It is missing from a copy of the model with random, untrained weights, so it was learnt.

  4. You play

    Switch a feature off, turn it up or force it on, and the rest of the model runs again on the edited patches. Blue shows where the model's final view of each patch moved. Playing is exploring: it never changes which features carry a tick.

Everything runs on your device with ONNX Runtime Web. Your pictures and camera never leave it. Model: facebook/dinov2-small (Apache 2.0), cut at block 6 of 12. Want to do this on your own model? Run ii and pack it with ii export-live.
